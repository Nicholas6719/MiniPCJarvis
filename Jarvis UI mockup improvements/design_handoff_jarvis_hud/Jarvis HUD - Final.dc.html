The complete redesign

One principle drives every decision below: the core is the subject, and it only ever leaves the middle of the screen to make room for something you have to read.

1920 × 1080 · VOICE FIRST RAJDHANI · SHARE TECH MONO 19 FRAMES · 13 CORE STATES NICHOLAS6719 / MINIPCJARVIS
WHAT CHANGED

The orb was a 220px decoration in a sidebar. It is now a 380–551px arc reactor at the centre of the screen that reacts to your voice and carries every piece of status.

WHAT WENT AWAY

Eleven unlabelled glyph tabs and the hover strip that hid them. Persistent status bars. The 44px grid. Nothing is navigated to any more.

WHAT ARRIVED

A stage that JARVIS drives — a real embedded Brave you can also drive, an image grid, your files — and a room that takes the machine's colour.

STILL TO DESIGN

Only the surfaces you haven't asked for yet. Everything in this document is specified.

01

The rule

Two geometries, and the state picks which one you get. Radial when the core is the subject — resting, listening, or something's broken. Anchor when reading is the subject — the core slides left and shrinks, and a stage opens beside it. Read the tags along the top: the core steps out of centre, then comes back.

THE HANDOFF

Core translates and scales over .9s on cubic-bezier(.65,0,.25,1). Rails and radial labels fade out, the stage slides in 44px. One easing for everything, so the core appears to carry the layout with it.

WHY FAULT IS RADIAL

Because coming back to the middle reads as the machine turning to face you. A failure isn't another panel appearing — it's the thing that was working stopping and looking at you.

02

The core

An arc reactor: twelve outer plates, eight coils, a charge arc, three spokes, and a triangle over a soft bloom. Thirteen states, and the mapping is deliberate — hue is the kind of state, ring speed is urgency, the charge arc is literal progress, coil deformation is live voice amplitude, and core brightness is whether it's speaking. Every moving part maps to one field. Nothing is decorative.

03

Resting, and listening

Rest is the screen you see 99% of the time, so it holds almost nothing: the core large and centred, one hint line, and chrome dimmed to a third. Listening keeps the exact same geometry — it hasn't got anything to show you yet — and changes only temperature: the room goes green, the coils come alive, the core tucks in slightly.

04

The stage

The right side is not a text column — it's one box, always in the same place, whose content type changes with the task. There is no Files view or Browser view to navigate to; the utterance picks the renderer. That's what replaced the eleven tabs.

The browser is a real embedded Brave with real tabs — you can click straight into it, open your own tab, and carry on while JARVIS finishes in its. Which makes one question load-bearing: who has the pointer right now. It's answered three ways at once so you can read it from anywhere on screen.

CYAN · JARVIS DRIVING

Its tab carries a coloured top edge and a live counter. A pinging marker sits on the link it's about to open.

WHITE · YOU HAVE IT

Badge and browser ring go white. JARVIS's tab keeps its cyan edge and its counter keeps climbing.

AMBER · WAITING ON YOU

A login wall or a confirmation. It hands over rather than stalling, and says what it still needs.

STAGE · PROSE — "how fast is this thing, really?"NOTHING TO LOOK AT, SO THE ANSWER IS THE CONTENT

Your question at 40px — deliberately the largest thing on screen. Source chips resolve green as they're read, the in-flight one flickers, a dashed chip counts what's queued. Underneath, the run reads left to right as a strip rather than a log, and the machine panel names the model and what it remembered about you.

STAGE · PROSE, SPEAKING — and the 5-second holdHOLDS, THEN COLLAPSES TO THE RESTING CORE

All six sources resolved, the queue gone, the run advanced to "Speaking it". Once it stops talking the stage holds five seconds and then collapses — signalled only by a thin draining bar, since almost always you'll just let it go. "Keep it" pins it, "keep it for ten minutes" sets a timer, "bring that back" restores the last stage whatever it was.

STAGE · BROWSER — "research the Iron Man arc reactor"JARVIS DRIVING

Full Brave chrome — back, forward, reload, address bar, tabs, and a + that's yours whenever you want it. Nothing is disabled; what's added is legibility. The working tab has a cyan top edge and a live 3/6, and the marker on result three is captioned OPENING RESULT 3 — you see the decision, not just the outcome. Page content is dimmed so a bright site can't blow out the room.

STAGE · BOTH AT ONCE — your tab in front, JARVIS still workingYOU HAVE THE POINTER

You clicked in, opened a second tab, and you're reading docs with a live caret — meanwhile tab 1 keeps its cyan edge and has ticked to 5/6. The header says STILL WORKING and names the tab it's in. Crucially the core on the left keeps charging: your progress indicator never disappears just because you looked away.

STAGE · HANDOFF — a login wall, so it gives you the pointerOVER TO YOU

It doesn't stall and it doesn't fail — it hands over. Room goes amber, core drops to NEEDS YOU, tab edge and browser ring turn amber, and the password field is pre-focused with an amber ring so your hands know where to go. Written across the bottom of the page and spoken: "It's yours — sign in and I'll pick up where I left off. The other five sources are already read." The escape hatch matters as much as the ask: say "skip it" and it answers from the five it has.

STAGE · IMAGES — "show me images of the arc reactor"JUST THE IMAGES, NO PAGE FURNITURE

You asked for images, so you get images — no titles, no snippets. Four across, source domain under each, the one it thinks you meant tagged BEST MATCH with a lit border, and the eighth still arriving and saying so. Voice takes it from there: "bigger", "the second one", "save that". Tiles are placeholders — real thumbnails come from the search.

STAGE · FILE — "what did I write about Vulkan speed?"TOUCHING FILES IS A TOOL RUN

Not a file browser and not a list of hits — the file, open, with the matching lines lit and everything around them dimmed to context. Line numbers so you can find it yourself later, path and edit time in the header, 3 OF 41 LINES MATCHED so you know how much it read. The room is amber rather than cyan, because the colour already told you this was a tool run.

STAGE · TWO AT ONCE — "research it and pull up my Vulkan notes"ONLY BECAUSE TWO TASKS ARE RUNNING

Split is a consequence, never a layout option. Two jobs, so two numbered panes, each with its own title, progress bar and content type — live browser left, file right. Pane 2 has finished and says DONE while pane 1 is on 3 of 6; the finished pane stays put so you can read it while the other works. Hard cap of two — a third queues rather than shrinking these.

05

When it breaks

Both faults use the same move — core back to centre, room goes red — but they carry different content, because the useful thing to say is different. A subsystem going down is about triage. You closing its tab is about how cheap it is to recover.

SUBSYSTEM DOWN — the vision server stopped answeringFOUR WEDGES · RADIAL

What's broken with a repair button, what's still healthy, live telemetry, and HAPPENED BEFORE — two prior occurrences and the observation that it's the third time today, so the driver is worth a look rather than another restart. Note telemetry shows idle numbers: the machine isn't busy, it's broken.

YOU CLOSED ITS TAB — mid-fetchTWO WEDGES · RECOVERABLE

Two wedges, not four, and the left one gets equal weight to the right: WHAT I STILL HAVE — five of six sources kept, notes intact, only the sixth lost. That's the line that makes the decision easy: "trying again only re-fetches the one, so it's about two seconds." It also says closed by you, not a crash, so you're not left wondering whether something's wrong with the machine.

06

First contact

Boot shows real sidecar events with real measurements — no fake progress bars anywhere. First run asks two questions at spoken scale and gets out of the way; no modal box, no wizard.

BOOTEVERY LINE IS A REAL EVENT

The core spinning up with its charge arc at 72%, then one row per subsystem: status dot, name, a real progress track, and a real timing. Done rows are green and full; the model is mid-load with an actual byte count; the rest sit queued at 45% opacity.

FIRST RUNTWO QUESTIONS, THEN OUT OF THE WAY

How it should listen, and which voice. Both at a size you'd read from a chair, each option carrying a plain-language subtitle rather than a setting name. The footer states the two rules that matter — web search stays off until you add a key, anything risky asks first — and then gets out of the way.

07

The rest of the stage

Media, tables, and settings — all three as stage types, none of them a destination you navigate to. On your questions: tables are for when the answer is a comparison and prose would make you hold four numbers in your head. Conversation history now lives inside settings as you asked, which turns out to be the right call — it's the same thing as "what have you got on me", so it sits next to memory and permissions.

And maps don't need a stage type at all — a map is a web page, so "how far is Portland" is the browser stage pointed at a map with the answer spoken over it. One less thing to build. Apps do need their own, so there's a sketch of that below.

STAGE · MEDIA — "put on Kid A"GREEN · NOTHING TO THINK ABOUT

Track title at 40px — same slot the answer occupies, because here the track is the answer. Transport is visible and clickable but it's not the point; the footer names the words that work instead. Up-next runs underneath so "skip two" makes sense. The room goes green rather than cyan: playing music needed no thinking, and a reflex handled it without waking the model. Video, incidentally, doesn't need its own type either — a local file plays in this frame with the art area becoming the picture, and anything on the web is already the browser stage.

STAGE · TABLE — "is it worth switching quant?"WHEN THE ANSWER IS A COMPARISON

This is what I meant by tables. Spoken aloud, eight quants × four numbers is unfollowable; side by side it's obvious. The row you're on is lit and tagged RUNNING, the winning number is the only one at 20px, and the last column is plain English rather than a metric. Then the actual answer sits under the table as a sentence — "stay where you are" — because a table alone isn't an answer. Same pattern would carry "what's eating my disk" or "what did I run this week".

STAGE · SETTINGS, ON HISTORY — "what did we talk about earlier?"HISTORY LIVES HERE NOW

Six sections in the rail, History selected. Each turn shows what you said, what it answered, and which tools ran as tags — including the one you denied at the gate, kept on the record on purpose. Voice reaches it directly: "settings, history", then "the Vulkan one". Note the core is at idle here, not working — it's showing you something, not doing anything, and the core tells the truth about that.

STAGE · APPS — "put the editor on the left"SPECULATIVE · YOUR CALL

You said this isn't needed yet, so here's just the shape of it: a live map of the screen with each window as a proportional tile, the one it just moved outlined and labelled, and everything else running listed beside it. It's the only surface here that shows you your machine's layout rather than content, which is why it can't borrow another type. The payoff would be named layouts — and "workshop mode" is already a taught command in your brain data, so the plumbing half exists.

08

The confirmation gate

You asked what this is. It already exists in your build — the thing that stops a high-risk tool from running until you say yes. Today it's a modal that prints the raw JSON arguments and offers ALLOW / DENY, which puts the burden of reading a code fragment on you at exactly the wrong moment.

Redesigned, it's a radial state — the core comes back to centre and faces you, amber, at NEEDS YOU — and it answers three questions in plain language: what will happen, exactly what it touches, and whether it can be taken back.

"DELETE THE OLD QUANT, THE THREE-BIT ONE"NOTHING HAS RUN YET
IT SAYS IT OUT LOUD

The spoken sentence carries the consequence, not the syntax: "that's 3.2 gigabytes and it does not go to the recycle bin." You can answer without looking up.

THE PATH IS STILL THERE

Left wedge keeps the literal target in mono, plus size, age, and — the line that prevents most accidents — other files touched: none.

SCOPED, NOT BLANKET

The third option is always allow deletes in D:\models — a folder, not a permission for everything forever. Dashed and quiet, so it isn't the easy path.

NO TIMEOUT, EVER

It waits indefinitely and never decays into yes. Walk away and come back an hour later and the file is still there. Amber holds until you answer.

10

Scaling

One rule, two clauses, and no breakpoints. Scale uniformly to fit, then let edge-anchored things use the real edges. Because this design is a centred core plus edge-anchored panels — not a flowing document — that gets you every screen without a single layout variant.

Nothing reflows, ever. Type stays proportional, so the hierarchy you approved at 1080p is the hierarchy at 4K and on a TV. What changes on a wider screen is breathing room, not composition.

CLAUSE ONE · THE FACTOR
scale = clamp(0.85,
  min(vw / 1920, vh / 1080),
  2.0)

Contain, not cover — so nothing is ever cropped. Applied once, as a transform on the frame root. The core, the stage, every panel and every type size comes along for free.

CLAUSE TWO · THE EDGES

After scaling, seven things re-anchor to the real viewport rather than the scaled box: the four corner ticks, the wordmark, the clock, and — the important one — the left and right wedge panels.

So on a 21:9 the wedges slide outward and the stage gets genuinely wider, instead of the whole composition sitting in a letterboxed island with black bars either side.

THE ONE EXCEPTION

The 0.85 floor exists because 10px mono × 0.85 is 8.5px, and below that the sublabels stop being readable at desk distance.

Under 1600 × 900, don't scale further — switch to compact: hide the mono sublabels, halve the wedge padding, drop the core to 1.15 at rest. That's the only layout variant in the whole system.

DISTANCE, NOT RESOLUTION

A 4K monitor at 60cm and a 4K TV at 3m have the same pixels and completely different needs, so resolution alone can't drive type size. One setting, two values:

DESKDefault. Scale as above, full density.
ROOM×1.25 on all type, mono sublabels off, wedges drop to two. Reads from a sofa.

Belongs in first run as a third question if you ever put it on a TV — otherwise leave it out and default to desk.

WHAT NOT TO DO
✕Media queries with per-breakpoint layouts. There is one layout; it scales.
✕Stretching to fill a wider screen. The core must stay circular and the plates concentric — never a non-uniform scale.
✕Letterboxing with black bars on ultrawide. Use the width.
✕Rem-based type with a fluid root. The transform already handles it, and doubling up drifts the ratios.
✓One transform on the root, seven edge-anchored exceptions, one compact flag. That's the whole implementation.
